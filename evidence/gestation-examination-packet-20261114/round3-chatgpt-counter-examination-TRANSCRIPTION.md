### 4. Interoception — new organ accepted

1,914 external analyst sweeps have reportedly been needed to understand the organism's internal condition — someone outside the organism knows more about its operating state than the organism itself. Distinction: external observability (an examiner can inspect the organism) vs internal interoception (the organism can determine its own condition through trustworthy measurements). ### 5. Nociception — new organ accepted

Interoception tells her what condition she is in; nociception tells her when something is going wrong. The Kubo failure is the example: the damage signal went to a human's phone; no demonstrated internal pathway recognised the failure and initiated an appropriate protective response. The damage-response pathway: organ experiences failure → damage is detected → severity and scope established (degradation vs catastrophic failure) → protective response (isolate, refuse, preserve, or request human intervention) → receipt and recovery assessment (incident and response independently inspectable). Critical distinction: nociception must not be confused with autonomous repair; recognition and safe response are hers, repair requires separate authority, resources and verification.
